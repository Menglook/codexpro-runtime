# Code of Conduct

## Our commitment

We are committed to a project community in which participation is respectful, professional, and free from harassment. This applies regardless of experience, identity, background, role, or preferred technical approach.

## Expected behavior

Participants are expected to:

- communicate with empathy and technical clarity;
- critique ideas and evidence rather than people;
- respect stated security, privacy, and public-boundary constraints;
- accept reasonable maintainer decisions and continue disagreements constructively;
- acknowledge mistakes and correct harmful or misleading information;
- avoid publishing personal, private, customer, credential, or vulnerability information;
- help make first contributions understandable and reviewable.

## Unacceptable behavior

Unacceptable behavior includes:

- harassment, threats, intimidation, stalking, or discriminatory conduct;
- sexualized language or unwanted sexual attention;
- personal attacks, insults, sustained disruption, or deliberate misrepresentation;
- publishing another person's private information without explicit permission;
- pressuring reporters to disclose security issues publicly;
- retaliating against a good-faith report;
- using project channels for spam, manipulation, or unrelated promotion.

## Scope

This Code applies in repository Issues, pull requests, reviews, discussions, project-linked community spaces, and public situations where someone is representing the project.

## Reporting

Until the project publishes a dedicated conduct address, submit sensitive Code of Conduct concerns through the repository's [private reporting form](https://github.com/Menglook/codexpro-runtime/security/advisories/new) and prefix the title with `[Conduct]`. Do not place conduct details, personal information, or security information in a public Issue.

Reports should include the relevant context, public links when safe, and the requested safety outcome. The maintainer will limit disclosure to what is needed to review and respond.

For software vulnerabilities, use the same private form but follow the technical reporting requirements in [SECURITY.md](SECURITY.md).

## Enforcement

The maintainer may take proportionate action, including clarification, a warning, content removal, temporary participation limits, or a permanent ban. Decisions should consider severity, impact, pattern, intent, remediation, and community safety.

When the sole maintainer is the subject of a serious report, the reporter may request review by a mutually acceptable experienced open-source maintainer. That reviewer can provide a non-binding assessment; repository-host enforcement remains with the repository owner or platform.
